The takeover

A second kind of gene begins inside the workings of the first, and ends up running them.

This is Cairns-Smith’s own scheme, redrawn. The first genes are simple enough to start unaided. Over many generations they come to make an apparatus around themselves, a box, and the box comes to make materials the bare Earth never could.

One of those materials happens to be copied from piece to piece. It did not arrive from outside, and it was not made in order to become a gene. It was useful for something else first. Once it carries information of its own, selection works on both kinds of gene at once, and the kind that does the work better gains ground.

No messages pass from the old genes to the new. What passes is the box.

After A. G. Cairns-Smith, Genetic Takeover and the Mineral Origins of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1982), figure 4.1 and pp. 120–124; and Seven Clues to the Origin of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1985), pp. 107–112. The drawing is new.
