The rope

A line of descent can change what it is made of without ever breaking.

What is handed down the generations is not one thing but a bundle. Members of the bundle are lost and others are added, and so long as enough of them overlap at every point, the line continues.

Nothing requires the newcomers to be made of the same material as the fibres they replace. A rope can begin as one stuff and end as another with no join anywhere along it. Cairns-Smith’s claim is that this happened to the material of heredity itself.

After A. G. Cairns-Smith, Genetic Takeover and the Mineral Origins of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1982), pp. 5 and 105–106; and Seven Clues to the Origin of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1985), pp. 61–62.
