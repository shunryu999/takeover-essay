The race

Four numbers decide which copier comes to fill the room: how fast, how many, how long, and how true.

A is the reference: one copy every two ticks, each copy exact, and no unit ever fails. B takes one tick. C makes two copies at a time. D’s units fail after copying twice. Half of E’s copies come out wrong.

None of the five is better built than the others in any other sense. The figure changes one number per lineage and lets the clock run. What a lineage is made of does not enter into it.

Engineers who design copying machines treat these as dials to be set. For anything that copies without a designer, they are what selection acts on.

After Robert A. Freitas Jr. and Ralph C. Merkle, Kinematic Self-Replicating Machines (Landes Bioscience, 2004), sections 5.1.1 (fecundity, fidelity and longevity, from Dawkins), 5.1.9 J1, J2 and L4, and 5.9.1.
