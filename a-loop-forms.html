A loop forms

Reactions branch through a mixture in every direction at once. Now and then one branch leads back to where it began.

Picture a mixture in which one ingredient is plentiful. Chemistry does not wait to be organised. Some of that ingredient reacts, and the products pile up.

As they pile up, reactions that were too rare to matter begin to count, and their products pile up in turn. The mixture ramifies in every direction at once. No step leads and none follows.

Almost all of these branches are dead ends. But every so often a product far along one branch happens to make something near its root. From then on that branch is a loop, and a loop that draws on its surroundings makes more of itself. Nothing else in the mixture does.

The idea of a self-feeding loop arising in a mixture of reactions follows work on autocatalytic sets: S. A. Kauffman, “Autocatalytic sets of proteins”, Journal of Theoretical Biology 119 (1986), 1–24.
