The arch

Some things cannot be built one piece at a time, and were built that way all the same. The trick is to take pieces away.

An arch is the simplest thing with this property. Every stone is held up by the others, so none can be placed first. Living chemistry is like that all the way through: each part is made by the others.

Cairns-Smith’s answer is that building need not only add. Put up something that can stand at every stage, a heap, and let the stones that will matter be laid over it. When they meet they hold each other, and whatever held them before can be removed. What remains looks impossible because the thing that made it possible has gone.

The move can be repeated. More stones go on top, another layer locks, and the first arch itself becomes removable.

After A. G. Cairns-Smith, Genetic Takeover and the Mineral Origins of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1982), pp. 95–99 and figures 3.3 and 3.4; and Seven Clues to the Origin of Life (Cambridge University Press, 1985), pp. 59–60. The drawing is new.
